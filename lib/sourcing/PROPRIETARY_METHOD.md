## THE THREE CORE PRINCIPLES

### 1. Target 80-90% Likelihood Zone
- NOT spray and pray (everyone with the title)
- NOT 100% perfect match (too competitive, unreachable, being recruited by everyone)
- **THE SWEET SPOT**: Qualified enough to do the job, reachable enough to actually respond

### 2. Messaging That Renders Response
- The preview is the ONLY free real estate
- Deliver intent in a glance
- Stand out from recruiter spam
- Create curiosity + credibility instantly

### 3. Self-Screening Through Assumptive Messaging
- Don't waste time qualifying everyone
- The message itself screens them
- If they respond, they're qualified AND interested
- If they don't respond, move on
- ~1% false positives (people who aren't sane) - filter quickly

## STEP 0: DEEP QUALIFICATION

Before sourcing begins, qualify the role completely. Incomplete specs = wasted effort.

### Technical/Role Specifications:

**For Engineering:**
- Specialization (frontend, backend, full stack, DevOps, mobile, embedded, etc.)
- Stack (JavaScript/React/Node, .NET/C#, Java/Spring, Python, Go, Ruby, etc.)
- Architecture knowledge (microservices, monolith, serverless, cloud-native)
- Seniority (junior, mid, senior, staff, principal, distinguished)
- Years of experience
- Specific technologies required

**For Sales:**
- Tenure (can they stick past 2 years? Bad salespeople get fired/quit quickly)
- Industry/Vertical (selling similar products = relationships + familiarity)
- Quota attainment history (consistently 80%+?)
- Deal sizes (SMB vs mid-market vs enterprise)
- Sales cycle length (transactional vs enterprise)
- Inbound vs outbound experience
- Sales methodology (MEDDIC, Challenger, Sandler, etc.)

**For Executive/Leadership:**
- Company stage experience (startup vs scale-up vs enterprise)
- Team size managed
- P&L responsibility ($XM budget)
- Board experience
- Successful exits/outcomes
- Industry transformations led
- Functional expertise

**For All Roles:**
- Location (on-site city, remote US-only, remote global, hybrid %)
- Visa requirements
- Company stage fit
- Industry experience needed

### Compensation & Benefits:
- Base salary range (floor to ceiling)
- Total compensation (equity, bonuses, etc.)
- Full benefits package details
- 401k match %
- Healthcare specifics
- PTO/vacation days
- Sign-on bonus availability
- Relocation assistance
- **Negotiation ranges** (absolute ceiling, walk-away floor)

### Company & Product Targeting:
- **Target companies**: Where are the right caliber candidates?
- **Avoid companies**:
  - Partnerships (don't poach from partners)
  - Bad product perception (reputation risk for candidate)
  - Legal/non-compete issues
  - Golden handcuffs/retention packages
  - Cultural mismatches

### Timeline & Process:
- When do they need to start?
- How many rounds of interviews?
- Decision timeline
- Urgency level

### The Critical Question:

**"How will this person be judged? What 3 things will determine their success or failure?"**

Use those 3 things as search keywords. Titles drift, but job outcomes don't.

**Example:**
- Role: VP of Sales
- Judged on: "Enterprise deal velocity, partner ecosystem building, board-level communication"
- Keywords: Search for people currently doing those three things, not just people with "VP Sales" title

## STEP 1: IDENTIFY THE 80-90% ZONE

The art is in targeting people who are qualified but reachable.

### Role-Specific Signal Sets:

#### Engineering (Objective/Technical Signals):
**100% Zone (Too Competitive - Avoid):**
- Working at FAANG in their prime tech stack
- Recently promoted
- Actively posting on tech Twitter/LinkedIn about loving their job
- Just joined (< 6 months tenure)
- Recent big equity refresher

**80-90% Zone (Sweet Spot):**
- Strong companies but not FAANG (mid-tier tech, successful startups)
- 18-36 months tenure (past honeymoon phase, not too settled)
- Right tech stack but maybe not cutting edge
- Contributing to open source but not a celebrity
- Career progression stalled slightly
- Compensation probably $130-160k when you're offering $150-180k (motivated by bump)

**50% Zone (Under-qualified - Avoid):**
- Junior when you need senior
- Wrong tech stack entirely
- Startups that failed or struggling companies
- Gaps in employment
- Job hopping (< 12 months average tenure)

#### Sales (Performance/Tenure Signals):
**100% Zone (Too Competitive - Avoid):**
- Crushing quota (150%+ attainment consistently)
- President's Club winners
- Recently promoted
- Huge territory/accounts
- Equity vesting soon

**80-90% Zone (Sweet Spot):**
- Tenure 2-4 years at current company (proven they can stick, but not too entrenched)
- Industry/vertical match (already selling similar products)
- Quota attainment 80-120% (solid but not superstar)
- Right deal size experience
- Might be hitting ceiling at current company
- Compensation bump opportunity

**50% Zone (Under-qualified - Avoid):**
- Can't stick past 18 months anywhere
- Different industry/vertical entirely
- Wrong deal size (SMB when you need enterprise)
- Missing quota consistently

#### Executive/Leadership (Results/Impact Signals):
**100% Zone (Too Competitive - Avoid):**
- C-suite at hot companies
- Recent successful exit
- Board seats at multiple companies
- Being actively recruited (visible on LinkedIn/Twitter)

**80-90% Zone (Sweet Spot):**
- VP/SVP at good companies (room to move to C-suite)
- Proven track record but not celebrity
- Company stage match
- Industry experience relevant
- Might be hitting ceiling
- Geographic/lifecycle fit

**50% Zone (Under-qualified - Avoid):**
- Wrong company stage (enterprise exec to startup or vice versa)
- No P&L experience when needed
- Team size mismatch
- Industry too different

## STEP 2: MESSAGING THAT RENDERS RESPONSE

### The Core Principle:

**THE PREVIEW IS THE ONLY FREE REAL ESTATE.**

Say what you want to say in a way that delivers intent in a glance.

### Subject Line Formula:

**Subject: [First Name]**

That's it. Nothing more, nothing less.

**Why:**
- Looks personal, not mass outreach
- Creates curiosity
- Stands out from spam ("Exciting opportunity!" / "Job opening at Google!")
- Forces them to read the preview to understand context

### Standard Message Formula:

```
Subject: [First Name]

I am working with [TEAM] at [COMPANY], they are looking for a [ROLE] to join the team in [LOCATION].

I found your profile both as someone knowledgeable in [DOMAIN/EXPERTISE] but also whom could potentially contribute to [COMPANY]'s [COMPELLING OUTCOME/GROWTH].

I assume you are happy where you are but if you are keeping your eyes and ears open to an opportunity, please circle back.
```

**Breakdown:**

**Line 1: WHO + WHAT + WHERE**
- Immediate credibility
- Not spam
- Clear intent in preview

**Line 2: WHY YOU SPECIFICALLY**
- Flattery + relevance
- Shows you actually looked at their profile
- Demonstrates this isn't mass mail

**Line 3: SOFT CLOSE**
- Assumes they're happy (low pressure)
- "Keeping eyes and ears open" = permission to be curious
- "Circle back" = easy ask

**Example:**
```
Subject: Sarah

I am working with the product team at Google, they are looking for a new Product Manager to join the team in NYC.

I found your profile both as someone knowledgeable in the product management space but also whom could potentially contribute to Google's explosive growth in AI/ML products.

I assume you are happy where you are but if you are keeping your eyes and ears open to an opportunity, please circle back.
```

### Confidential Variation:

When replacing someone still employed, or client wants stealth mode:

```
Subject: [First Name]

I am working with a client disrupting [INDUSTRY/SPACE] and they are looking for a [ROLE] to join the team in [LOCATION].

I found your profile both as someone knowledgeable in [DOMAIN] but also whom could potentially contribute to [COMPELLING OUTCOME].

I assume you are happy where you are but if you are keeping your eyes and ears open to an opportunity, please circle back.
```

**Why this variation works:**
- Creates curiosity ("Who is it?")
- Sometimes MORE effective than named company
- Protects confidentiality
- Makes them ask "tell me more"

### Message Customization Variables:

**[TEAM]** = "the engineering team" / "the product team" / "the sales leadership"
**[COMPANY]** = Actual company name (Google, Stripe, etc.)
**[ROLE]** = Exact role title (Senior Backend Engineer, VP of Sales, etc.)
**[LOCATION]** = "in NYC" / "remotely" / "in SF Bay Area"
**[DOMAIN/EXPERTISE]** = What they're good at based on profile
**[COMPELLING OUTCOME]** = "explosive growth" / "mission to X" / "expansion into Y"

## STEP 3: SELF-SCREENING THROUGH ASSUMPTIVE MESSAGING

**The Principle: The message itself IS the screening.**

### How It Works:

**If you say:**
> "I found your profile both as someone knowledgeable in hardware engineering..."

**And they're NOT a hardware engineer:**
- They won't respond (self-select OUT)
- Sane people don't apply for jobs they're not qualified for

**And they ARE a hardware engineer:**
- If interested → They respond (self-select IN)
- If not interested → They don't respond (self-select OUT)

### The Result:

You ONLY talk to people who are:
1. ✅ Qualified (or they wouldn't respond)
2. ✅ Interested (or they wouldn't respond)

### The 1% Exception:

~1% of people are not sane and will respond saying "Yes I'm interested!" even though they have zero relevant experience.

**How to handle:**
- Quick 5-minute phone screen
- Ask 1-2 basic qualifying questions
- Filter out immediately
- Don't waste more time

### No Qualifying Calls Needed:

**Traditional recruiting:**
- Blast 100 people
- 20 respond
- Spend hours screening to find 5 qualified + interested
- Waste 15 hours on unqualified people

**This method:**
- Target 30 people in 80-90% zone
- 10 respond
- All 10 are qualified + interested (minus 1 crazy person)
- Zero wasted time

## EXECUTION WORKFLOW

### Phase 1: Qualification Call with Hiring Manager

**Goal:** Get complete role specification

**Questions to ask:**
1. Technical/role specifics (see Step 0)
2. Comp/benefits details
3. Target/avoid companies
4. Timeline
5. **"How will this person be judged? What 3 things?"**

**Output:** Complete role specification document

### Phase 2: Candidate Identification

**Goal:** Build list of 80-90% zone candidates

**Process:**
1. Use "3 judgment criteria" as search keywords
2. Apply role-specific signals (engineering, sales, executive)
3. Check target/avoid company lists
4. Verify compensation reachability
5. Verify location match

**Output:** List of 20-50 candidates in 80-90% zone

### Phase 3: Outreach

**Goal:** Message candidates using formula

**Process:**
1. Personalize message for each candidate:
   - Subject: [First Name]
   - Line 1: WHO + WHAT + WHERE
   - Line 2: WHY YOU (based on their actual profile)
   - Line 3: SOFT CLOSE
2. Send via LinkedIn InMail or email (if you have it)
3. Track responses

**Output:** 20-40% response rate (if targeting is good)

### Phase 4: Response Handling

**Goal:** Qualify interest and set up interviews

**Process:**
1. Response comes in → Assume qualified + interested
2. Quick reply with more details (if confidential, reveal client now)
3. Gauge interest level
4. If high interest → Share full details, schedule call
5. If medium interest → Nurture, follow up in 2 weeks
6. If crazy person (1%) → Politely decline

**Output:** 3-7 qualified candidates for client to interview

### Phase 5: Interview Coordination

**Goal:** Get candidates through client's process

**Process:**
1. Prep candidate for interview
2. Prep client on candidate background
3. Coordinate schedules
4. Follow up after each round
5. Handle objections/questions

**Output:** 1-2 offers made

### Phase 6: Offer Negotiation

**Goal:** Close the candidate

**Process:**
1. Present offer
2. Negotiate using pre-defined ceiling/floor
3. Handle counter-offers from current employer
4. Get signed offer letter
5. Support through resignation/transition

**Output:** Accepted offer, start date confirmed

## RESPONSE RATE TARGETS

### Good Targeting:
- 20-40% response rate
- 50%+ of responders are qualified + interested
- 10-15% conversion to interview
- 30-50% offer acceptance rate

### Poor Targeting:
- <10% response rate (wrong zone, wrong messaging)
- <30% of responders are qualified (messaging not assumptive enough)
- <5% conversion to interview (wrong candidates)

### Diagnosis:

**Low response rate:**
- Check: Are you in 80-90% zone or chasing 100%?
- Check: Is messaging formula followed correctly?
- Check: Subject line = first name only?

**High response but low qualification:**
- Check: Is Line 2 assumptive enough? (should screen people out)
- Check: Are you being too generic?

**High qualification but low offer acceptance:**
- Check: Compensation competitive?
- Check: Are you overselling and under-delivering?
- Check: Counter-offer strategy
